How it’s calculated, with sources
Body mass index
Weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. The adult reference range is 18.5 to 24.9. Source
Body fat by tape measure
The U.S. Navy circumference method: height, neck and abdomen for men, and height, neck, waist and hips for women. This estimate only appears in the body-composition card and does not change your calories. Source
Body-scan reading
Optional. With a body-fat reading, resting calories use the Cunningham equation on fat-free mass (370 + 21.6 × fat-free kg) instead of Mifflin–St Jeor. Body-fat ranges by sex and age come from Gallagher and colleagues. Skeletal-muscle ranges follow Omron’s tables and differ between devices. When body fat is above the healthy range, protein is based on your weight at the top of that range with the same fat-free mass. Scan readings are estimates and shift with hydration and time of day. Source: Cunningham 1991, Gallagher 2000
Daily calories
The Mifflin–St Jeor equation estimates resting calories from age, height, weight and sex. An activity factor turns that into maintenance calories. The fat-loss goal subtracts 500 kcal, with a floor of 1,500 for men and 1,200 for women, and the muscle goal adds 300 kcal. These are starting points to adjust as you progress. Source
Macronutrient split
Protein is 1.6 g per kg to maintain, 1.8 to build muscle and 2.0 to lose fat, within the 1.4 to 2.0 g per kg range the International Society of Sports Nutrition recommends for people who train. Fat is 25% of calories and the rest is carbohydrate. Protein and carbohydrate provide 4 kcal per gram, fat 9. Source
Daily water
A common rule of thumb: 30 to 35 ml per kg of body weight a day. For comparison, the U.S. National Academies’ reference for total daily water from food and drinks is 3.7 L for men and 2.7 L for women. Source
Heart-rate zones
The Tanaka equation subtracts 0.7 times age from 208. Zones are percentages of that estimate, and your actual heart rate can differ. Source
One-rep max
The Epley equation: weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30) for a set of 2 to 12 reps. For one rep the result is the weight you entered. Source

General estimates for adults, not medical advice. If you have a health condition or are pregnant, speak with your doctor first.
